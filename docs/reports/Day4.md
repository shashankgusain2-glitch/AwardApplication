Today's discovery session clarified how the awards program is organised, how registrations were handled in the past, how judging works and how results are published. The aim was to ask the right questions so the problem statement is understood correctly before design begins.

2. Key Findings from the Meeting
   2.1 Organisation Structure
   Level	Role	Responsibility
   1	Leader	Heads the program and oversees all members
   2	Members	Report to the leader; each member is assigned one award to manage
   3	Working staff	Support the member in running that award

Key point: Each award has one accountable member, supported by a dedicated staff team.

2.2 Registration (Previous Approach)
Manual: Participants contact the award organiser, request details and send their information for evaluation and rating.
Automated: Some platforms provide an online registration flow that collects entries directly.

Implication: The new system should support the automated flow while allowing organisers to enter registrations manually where needed.

2.3 Judging Process

Judge selection

Judges are chosen by the organising committee, or
A higher manager recommends judges based on the requirements of the specific award.

Scoring method

Judges do not interact with each other.
Each judge scores independently.
Individual scores are averaged to produce the final score.

Judging rounds

A second round applies only to some awards.
Where used, the process is:
Round 1 scoring is completed.
A shortlist of teams is selected.
Shortlisted teams are called together for a live competition in Round 2.
Each team presents why it deserves the award.

Implication: The number of rounds (1 or 2) should be configurable per award.

2.4 Anonymity
Full anonymity is not required for all awards.
Some awards demand complete anonymity, and for those it must be applied fully.

Implication: Anonymity should be a configurable setting per award, not a global rule.
